# Evidence for the Viral Direction Framework

Retrieved 2026-11-04 by web search (standard mode, one extended search for the video leaderboards). Most claims were read from search-result
summaries and abstracts. Where a source page was not opened, that is said. Before any figure here is quoted outside Meridian, open its
source and check it. This note extends `docs/engine/RESEARCH_SHORT_FORM_2026-10.md`, which covers the platforms' hook guidance; that note's
findings are not repeated here.

Labels are the framework's: `sourced`, `practice`, `hypothesis`, `contested` (see the framework, section 0).

## 6. Not found, or not checked

- No peer-reviewed study of the first three seconds of short-form video retention (as in the October note).
- No official retention benchmarks for Shorts, Reels or TikTok.
- No controlled study of loop seams or re-hook intervals in short video. Both rules are `practice` or `hypothesis`.
- No independent replication of Verbalized Sampling.
- No current, audio-inclusive video-understanding leaderboard.
- The primary texts of the TikTok newsroom post, the Mosseri statements, and YouTube's policy page were read only through secondary
  reports.
